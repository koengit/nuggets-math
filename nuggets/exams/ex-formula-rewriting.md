(a) Give the truth table of $(p \vee q) \Leftrightarrow p$. Does it mean the same as $q \Rightarrow p$? Explain.

(b) Give the truth table of $(p \wedge q) \vee (p \vee q)$. Find a formula that means the same, in which $p$ and $q$ each occur only once.

(c) Give the truth table of $(\neg p \wedge \neg q) \vee q$. Find a formula that means the same and uses **exactly one** of the connectives $\wedge, \vee, \Rightarrow, \Leftrightarrow$ (and $p$, $q$, $\neg$ as often as you like).

(d) The same for $(\neg p \vee \neg q) \wedge q$.

## Hint

Make the truth table first, then compare its last column with the tables of $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\Rightarrow$, $\Leftrightarrow$ applied to $p$, $\neg p$, $q$, $\neg q$. Recall $A \Rightarrow B \equiv \neg A \vee B$ ([[logical-laws]]).
