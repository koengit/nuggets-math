A formula is **valid** if it is always true. Which of these are valid? Justify with truth tables.

(a) $(p \wedge q) \Rightarrow (p \vee q)$
(b) $(p \vee q) \Rightarrow (p \wedge q)$
(c) $(p \wedge q) \vee (\neg p \wedge \neg q)$

## Hint

Write one row per combination of $p, q$ (four rows) and a column for every subformula. A single row with a 0 in the last column is enough to show a formula is *not* valid. What does (c) say in words?
