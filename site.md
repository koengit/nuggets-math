This is a collection of **nuggets**: small, self-contained pieces of discrete mathematics. A nugget is one idea — a definition with examples, a theorem with its proof, an alternative proof, a worked example, or an exercise with an answer.

**How to use it.**

- Browse by **topic**, or follow a **lecture**, which reads as a short path through the nuggets.
- Every nugget links to what it *assumes you know*, what it *uses*, what to *read more* about, and *natural next topics*.
- Mark each nugget: **I understand this**, **I want to go back to this**, or **I need to know more about this**, and write your own notes. Everything is stored in your browser; use [My notes](#/me) to see it all and to move it to another browser with an export link.
- Press **+ compose** on nuggets to collect them into one page, and save that as your own collection.
- In exercises, answers are hidden until you click **Show answer**. Try first!

The main book is *Mathematics for Computer Science* by Lehman, Leighton and Meyer (MCS); for sets and functions also *Lemurell and Jonasson*.
